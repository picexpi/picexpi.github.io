1. Executive Summary

picex is a hybrid exchange designed for the Pi Network ecosystem. Its goal is to connect professional market experience with the accessibility, identity and settlement capabilities of the Pi ecosystem.

The product strategy is built on two core pillars:

  • A high-speed off-chain layer for order matching, order book management and market execution.
  • A Pi-based trust and settlement layer for user identity, payment flows, final registration and settlement where applicable.

Official Pi SDK documentation emphasizes that developers can use native ecosystem capabilities to authenticate users with Pi accounts, manage payments, and interact with standardized Pi Wallet and server-side payment validation flows. In parallel, Pi Network’s official whitepaper and roadmap highlight the importance of practical utilities, KYC, migration and ecosystem growth.

In this context, picex is not designed as a simple dashboard or a fully on-chain DEX that repeats the limitations of congested settlement layers. Instead, it is designed as real market infrastructure: a transparent order book, market and limit orders, professional pricing paths, market-maker incentives, and user access levels based on KYC.

picex aims to provide an exchange-grade trading experience while keeping identity, access control and final trust aligned with the logic of the Pi ecosystem.

2. Problem Statement

Digital markets become efficient only when three conditions exist at the same time:

  • Low latency execution.
  • Complete and professional trading tools.
  • Deep and aggregated liquidity.

In many fully decentralized structures, congestion and settlement delay at the on-chain layer make professional order execution difficult. As a result, price efficiency declines, volatility increases, slippage rises, and the attractiveness of daily trading activity is reduced.

On the other hand, when tools such as a deep order book, limit orders, predictable pricing routes and liquidity incentives do not exist, everyday use of Pi for trading remains limited for both ordinary users and active traders.

Pi Network’s own official documentation is built around the idea that the ecosystem should be practical, broad and useful for developers. The Pi Whitepaper introduces the network as a utility-based peer-to-peer ecosystem, and the official roadmap shows that the Enclosed Mainnet phase was designed around KYC, migration and building useful applications.

This creates a clear need for a professional trading layer. If users and assets exist in a widely distributed network, there must also be an infrastructure capable of aggregating liquidity, controlling access, and preserving speed at the same time.

picex is designed to fill this gap: an exchange that is compatible with professional market behavior and also aligned with the logic of the Pi ecosystem.

3. picex Hybrid Architecture

The architecture of picex consists of two layers. Each layer is responsible for a clear part of the trading cycle.

3.1 Off-Chain Matching Engine

The first layer is the off-chain matching engine. This engine processes orders in memory and within low-latency infrastructure. It is responsible for:

  • Order registration.
  • Order prioritization.
  • Order book state management.
  • Real-time order book updates.
  • Market and limit order execution.
  • Professional-scale matching.

This design separates the execution path from the limitations of direct interaction with a congested chain. It enables real-time order books, faster execution, precise state management and a trading experience closer to professional exchanges.

3.2 On-Chain Settlement Layer

The second layer is the settlement layer. In this layer, final ownership control and asset movement are handled through Pi ecosystem capabilities where applicable.

Official Pi SDK documentation states that the SDK is designed for user authentication, User-to-App and App-to-User payments, native interaction with Pi Wallet, and standardized server-side integration patterns. In the language of the picex architecture, Pi is not only a visual identifier; it is used as a layer of identity and trust.

Even though orders are matched quickly off-chain, the final transaction logic is designed to rely on verifiable flows, user identity and asset movement rules connected to the Pi ecosystem.

The core advantage of this model is balance: speed and user experience are optimized off-chain, while final trust and validation remain connected to the settlement and identity path of the Pi ecosystem.

4. Access Model and Fee Economy

The picex economic model is based on the assumption that greater access should be accompanied by greater trust. For this reason, the fee structure is built around two main user levels:

  • Unverified users.
  • KYC-Verified users.
User Level Fee Logic Access Logic Purpose
Unverified Higher fees Lower limits and more operational restrictions Risk control and compensation for additional compliance overhead
KYC-Verified Significantly lower fees Higher limits and broader platform access Encourage trust, compliance, long-term participation and higher trading volume
Market Makers Discounts or rebates Special market-making status based on liquidity contribution Maintain deep order books and reduce slippage

At the Unverified level, fees remain higher so that risk, operational restrictions and withdrawal controls can be properly managed. At the KYC-Verified level, fees decrease meaningfully to give verified users an incentive to trade more actively, hold assets longer and remain compliant.

Market makers also require a special position within this structure. Participants who continuously provide liquidity can receive fee discounts or rebates so that order book depth is preserved. This policy is essential for a hybrid exchange because a fast matching engine without sufficient liquidity only increases the speed at which market gaps appear.

The economic goal of picex is not simply “low fees.” The goal is a structure that rewards liquidity providers, reduces slippage, and gives ordinary users a clear path toward higher account status.

5. $PIC Token and the In-Platform Economy

The $PIC token is designed as a coordination tool for the in-platform economy of picex. It is not intended to be merely a speculative asset or a decorative token on the interface.

5.1 Fee Reduction

The first function of $PIC is fee reduction. Holding or locking $PIC can create discounts of up to 30% on trading fees, allowing active users to return part of the generated value back into the platform cycle.

5.2 Governance

The second function is governance. $PIC holders can participate in voting on new asset listings, protocol updates and important platform decisions. This moves the system away from a fully centralized structure and gives active community members meaningful influence.

5.3 Buyback and Burn

The third function is an anti-inflationary policy. Allocating 20% of net fees to periodic buyback and burn can reduce supply pressure and create a direct connection between platform revenue growth and token scarcity.

5.4 Staking and Liquidity Locking

The fourth function is staking. Users can lock $PIC in liquidity pools to receive rewards while also strengthening their trading level. This model encourages long-term behavior and helps align users, traders, market makers and governance participants.

At the product design level, $PIC should directly connect to liquidity generation, cost reduction and participation in decision-making.

6. KYC, Security and Pi SDK-Based Settlement

Security in picex consists of three layers:

  • Identity validation.
  • Transaction validation.
  • Asset control.

Pi SDK documentation explains that the SDK provides a foundation for secure user login, native Pi payments, wallet interaction and server APIs. This is important for picex because an exchange can only be trusted when user identity and payment events are validated on the server side, not only on the client side.

At the KYC level, users must complete identification processes to reach Verified status. This requirement is not only for legal compliance; it is also necessary for reducing fraud, managing risk, enforcing different withdrawal limits and improving the overall safety of the platform.

From a custody and settlement perspective, the logic must be designed so that user assets remain under secure control until the final settlement moment. Final transfer should only happen after consistency is verified between balance, order status and settlement state.

In picex, security is not only a technical layer. It is part of the access model and the economic model: the more verified and trustworthy a user is, the lower the cost and the greater the available functionality.

7. Technical Stack and Operational Requirements

For a hybrid exchange, the technical stack must serve reliability, scalability and operational clarity. picex can be designed with:

  • Node.js and TypeScript for the service layer.
  • PostgreSQL for transactional data.
  • Prisma for schema management and migrations.
  • Docker for service separation and deployment consistency.
  • Nginx for routing, load balancing and production delivery.

This combination is suitable for a system that requires both development speed and data integrity. From the perspective of Pi ecosystem integration, Pi SDK acts as the main gateway for user login and interaction validation.

From an operational perspective, the backend must be designed for:

  • Audit trails.
  • Error logging.
  • Monitoring and observability.
  • Matching engine state checks.
  • Settlement layer coordination.
  • Balance and order state reconciliation.

In an exchange with a live order book, even a small mismatch between off-chain state and on-chain assets can become an operational risk. Therefore, observability and control are inseparable parts of the architecture.

8. Execution Roadmap

The picex roadmap can be divided into four logical phases.

Phase 1 — Infrastructure

Define the architecture, implement the backend, set up PostgreSQL and Prisma, and establish the initial Pi SDK connection for login and authorization. Security and compliance frameworks should also be stabilized in this phase so that the product is built on trust from the beginning.

Phase 2 — Spot Market

Launch the matching engine, order book, market and limit orders, and secure settlement flows. At this point, picex can move from an infrastructure system into a real market platform.

Phase 3 — Derivatives

Introduce perpetual futures, margin logic and a liquidation engine. This stage should only be launched when liquidity control, real-time risk monitoring and operational stability have reached sufficient maturity.

Phase 4 — Ecosystem Expansion

Expand staking, governance or DAO functionality, advanced APIs and deeper institutional liquidity access. The official Pi roadmap also shows that the network moves in the direction of ecosystem growth and utility development. Therefore, picex should prioritize market depth and operational stability instead of rushed expansion.

9. Conclusion

picex is not intended to be just another exchange. The project aims to build the professional trading layer of the Pi ecosystem.

Its primary value comes from combining three elements:

  • Fast off-chain execution.
  • Pi-based identity and settlement logic.
  • A fee economy that rewards liquidity and verified participation.

Based on the Pi SDK and official Pi documentation, this approach is aligned with the logic of the ecosystem and can evolve from a simple user experience into a deep and sustainable market.

picex is designed to become a professional, secure and liquidity-aware trading infrastructure for Pi Network users, developers, traders and market makers.
